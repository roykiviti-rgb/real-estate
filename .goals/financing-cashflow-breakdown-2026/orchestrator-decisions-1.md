## D1 — 2559 Q2: `תשלום לבעל עניין בגין התאמת תמורה` (52,500) stays in item 1

The payment was made to an IPO investor (הפניקס) as a price adjustment for its participation in the
share issuance, and it is charged to equity. Economically it reduces the net proceeds of the share
issuance. R3 item 1 already nets share-issuance costs paid into item 1 (as a negative amount).

R3 lists contingent-consideration payments under `אחר`, but that refers to business-combination
consideration, which this is not.

Therefore:
- keep the line in item 1;
- the line note must state the reasoning in Hebrew;
- the user will be told explicitly in the final summary that this is a judgment call that can be
  moved to `אחר`.

## D2 — Mixed short-/long-term borrowing lines follow phase-C rule A literally

A gross borrowing line that cannot be attributed to a single balance-sheet class goes to **4/7**,
with a note citing the evidence. Examples are lines that include long-term project or land loans and
short-term credit (1905 `קבלת/פירעון אשראי`, 2386 `קבלת/פירעון אשראי והלוואות מגופים פיננסיים`).
Item 5 applies only when the evidence shows the line's borrowings are short-term (current) credit.
This is the reading the Inspector applied (batch 7, B1/B2), and it matches how the other mixed cases
were mapped (1829, 486, 1797, 2360).

## D3 — Sign of the (\*) info rows (Phase C rule D) is confirmed

- A single source line keeps its printed sign.
- A row that combines a statement line printed in parentheses with an appendix amount printed
  without parentheses shows the appendix component with the payment sign (negative). This applies
  to 121 and 1083.
- The note on the row, the line note in `מיפוי שורות`, and the master `הסבר` sheet must all say so.
